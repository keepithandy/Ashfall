The destination

What Ashfall is becoming

A level-based survival-horror game that feels open: connected, believable areas with multiple routes, discovery, environmental storytelling, dangerous anomalies and threats that can be evaded through awareness and real cover. Preserve the mood of isolation, vulnerability and exploration while giving Ashfall its own creatures and identity.

Explore places

Checkpoint, cottages, Crossroads, research and relay each have a purpose, distinct contents, landmarks and reasons to return.

Survive encounters

Enemies notice sight and sound, pursue, search and give up. Clear cues and solid cover give players understandable choices.

Build on what exists

Keep existing inventory, all eight items, movement, sound settings, anomalies, Crossroads and authored source assets.

Planning assumptions, not commitments: a small independent team, primarily Windows PC, single-player and a fictional late-1980s rural-industrial district. Team size, weekly hours, funding, final playtime, number of areas, release platform and combat scope have not been confirmed. Milestone dates are targets, not promises.

Proposed scope: one complete first district and one additional distinct area, provided the first district proves the schedule. Set the final number of areas and game length after the vertical slice and capacity review. Avoid promising an open-world-sized map without evidence of available time and resources.

Where we are

The real starting point

Playable foundation

Crossroads and North Relay have a connected session-based journey, existing inventory/items, anomalies, objectives and a pursuit/search prototype. Areas currently remain resident; restarting resets progress.

Art foundation

Ten concept boards, actual source renders, five building briefs and a reuse/credits record establish the first visual direction. Concepts are proposals rather than finished models.

Known gaps

Disk saves, complete human playthroughs, broader navigation coverage, production creature animation and Blender source compatibility are outstanding. Some bathroom and household details remain unresolved.

See the existing playable-slice notes and visual art review. The prior test history is useful evidence, not proof that all current experience is finished or manually verified.

Work happening across the schedule

The full development workstreams

World & level design

Map every area around a function, multiple approaches, clear landmarks and at least one optional discovery. Keep area identities distinct. Validate loops, transitions and return reasons before decoration. Record collision, sightlines and places to hide; make hazards readable at walking height.

Exploration & objectives

Preserve the existing sample/extraction and Crossroads/relay objectives. Establish a clear critical path and optional stories, without making the player read every note. Maintain useful landmarks and the receiver. Track objective state across visits and saves.

Threats & fairness

Specify sight cones, occlusion, sound sources, investigation, pursuit, last-known-position search and eventual disengagement. Test quiet/loud movement, walls, doorways, corners, stairs, hiding, failed paths and attacks. Control encounter frequency and spawn locations so uncertainty remains fair.

Player, items & survival

Keep existing movement, all eight items, grips, readable inventory and sound settings. Test hunger/health/stamina/light economy against real playtime before changes. Treat pistol/rifle and any combat as a decision gate; appearance research alone does not approve combat.

Anomalies

Keep existing environmental hazards and meter. Establish visual/audio warning, readable range, consistent consequences and routes around obstacles. Check accessibility in dim light and avoid misleading concept labels as implementation specifications.

Art, Blender & assets

Use the late-1980s fictional district, existing Crossroads/cottage sources and a shared material family. Research and check license before creating a replacement. Record author, license, editable source, scale, compatibility and version; adapt selectively. Review younger Blender-version warnings before saving/exporting.

Audio & atmosphere

Build from existing settings and synthesized footsteps, breathing, creaks and generator. Add identifiable idle/notice/chase/search cues and material-specific environmental audio. Balance with headphones and speakers; add independent volume controls, subtitles for meaningful cues and an accessibility alternative.

Story & identity

Write a concise premise, timeline, location histories and an ending. Give households and workspaces a former purpose; use objects, signs and optional records to show what happened. Keep the setting and creature identity original.

Engineering & saves

Keep area/object IDs stable, use versioned save data and safe writes, and test across fresh starts, transitions, restarts and updates. Decide resident areas versus streaming from measured needs. Keep reusable source files and avoid overwriting hand edits with builders.

Testing & performance

Maintain small automated regression checks for movement, inventory, use, saves, area links, objectives, anomalies and enemy visibility/navigation. Add human full playthroughs, cold first-time testing and hardware profiling. Log steps, severity, expected/actual behavior and verification.

Accessibility & usability

Plan remappable controls if feasible, readable scaling/contrast, audio sliders, meaningful sound subtitles or visual alternatives, brightness guidance and motion settings. Test at actual display size and document limitations; do not imply features are already implemented.

Business & release

Choose store/platforms, entity, budget, ratings, localization and pricing with the owner. Plan credits, third-party licenses, user-data handling, business/tax and release support. Public store pages, demos, outreach and publishing require a separate owner decision and authorization.

Making progress measurable

Testing and completion standards

Every change

Check the feature in isolation and the existing related behavior. Record a short note on results and risks.

Every month

Play the latest build, review progress against capacity, track serious bugs, back up source work and revise priorities.

Every milestone

Run automated checks and a separate human playthrough. Observe at least one fresh player at major experience gates when possible.

Test the player journey

  1. Start a new game, understand controls and find the first objective without developer guidance.
  2. Visit each connected area by intended transitions; inspect alternate routes and hazards.
  3. Pick up, carry, use and retain each item type where applicable. Save, quit, reload and revisit.
  4. Exercise threat absent/present cases; test sight and sound, solid walls, hiding, pursuit, search and disengagement.
  5. Complete Crossroads, relay and research objectives, reach the ending, and verify rewards and one-time events.
  6. Repeat at different sound/brightness settings and on the agreed minimum and recommended hardware.

Performance targets are to be measured and agreed in Q1. Record frame rate and worst spikes, loading times, memory use and test hardware. Godot recommends profiling to find actual bottlenecks and measuring on more than one device where possible. Godot performance guidance.

Keeping the plan honest

Capacity, budget and decisions

Before setting a fixed launch date, record available hours per week, contributors and responsibilities. The same 24 calendar months can support very different scope depending on whether development is part-time, full-time or shared across specialists.

Budget categories to estimate

People and specialist help; software/hardware; commercial assets and licenses; original audio and voice; testing devices; localization and accessibility; business, ratings and store fees; marketing; and a contingency for rework. No amounts are assumed until scope and resources are known.

If time is tighter than expected: retain a complete beginning-to-ending game in the first district; reduce extra locations, optional props, creature variants and localization scope before cutting saves, readable threat cues, testing or an ending.
Start here

The next 30 days

  1. Walk and map the existing district: document the actual routes, entrances, bottlenecks and area links; mark what already exists and what is proposed.
  2. Make a player-view board: checkpoint approach, a cottage interior, Crossroads yard/escape, research approach and relay return. Show visibility and cover, not just attractive buildings.

  4. Resolve the critical art gaps: bathroom/household fixtures, real furniture fit and source compatibility. Keep the existing source ledger up to date.
  5. Agree practical constraints: owner’s weekly availability, budget, intended platform and target experience length; use those answers to revise the provisional schedule and game scope.

At the end of the month, review the district map and artwork together. Then choose the first approved production tasks and establish a baseline playthrough and performance measurement.
