A district with a lived-in center

The proposed setting is a fictional, temperate rural district in the late 1980s. A road passes a checkpoint, scattered homes and a maintenance station; woods and utility land surround them. A remote relay and research site extend the sense of place beyond the settlement.

How to read this map: It is a design sketch, not a measured map or a claim that all these connections exist in the game. Existing gameplay uses a powered service passage to North Relay; the wooded trail and other connections below are proposals to evaluate. The map does not prescribe compass directions or distances.

Solid line: the existing powered service passage between the Crossroads area and North Relay. Dashed lines: proposed or not-yet-verified connections. Settlement and research positioning is illustrative; verify all physical links against the game before building.

Place, purpose, return

What the player finds

Existing entry and report point

Checkpoint

Inspection barriers, records and an improvised rest area introduce the district. The gate is a recognizable place to return with reports; existing rewards support that loop.

Question to resolve: Who is receiving the reports, and why?

Cottages exist · settlement layout proposed

Homes and lanes

Give a small group of homes different histories: a maintained household, a shift worker’s room and signs of someone preparing to leave. A road and back yards offer orientation and alternate cover.

Return reason, proposed: a clue found elsewhere identifies a household cache or useful record. Its contents and access remain to be designed.

Existing station and objective

Crossroads

The maintenance station and yard explain how the district was kept running. Starting the generator creates noise; the player waits, retrieves its diagnostic and powers the relay passage.

Return reason: report the diagnostic at the entry gate; a later station revisit is a design opportunity, not yet a defined objective.

Existing early area

North Relay

A cottage and service yard sit on the edge of the district. The recorder remains on the rear workbench. One side passes an anomaly; the other offers supplies. A threat may be present.

Return reason: carry the recorder back to the entry gate. The immediate service passage and its lock already exist.

Existing sample mission · placement proposed

Research approach

Receiving, examination and records give the research presence a practical identity. The existing sample-and-extraction mission provides a destination; its exact position and connections need an in-game audit.

Return reason: bring the sample to extraction in the existing mission. The route and story link to the relay are proposals.

Proposed connecting landscape

Woods and service land

Tree lines, drainage, damaged fences and utility markers can make the district feel larger without relying on a huge empty map. Landmarks should help players navigate even when visibility is poor.

Tradeoff: a quieter wooded route could offer concealment but expose the player to an anomaly or longer travel. Exact layout and encounter rules are undecided.

A first playable journey

How the district could unfold

  1. Enter and orient. From the checkpoint, the player sees the road and signs of the people who once used it. Nearby homes invite exploration without obscuring the main route.
  2. Make a costly noise. At Crossroads, the player starts the generator, finds cover during warmup and retrieves the diagnostic. This reflects the existing objective; its pacing still needs a human playthrough.
  3. Reach the relay. The powered service passage takes the player to North Relay. Choose between the anomaly-side lane and the supply yard, then recover the recorder from the rear workbench.
  4. Return with new context. Bring the diagnostic and recorder to the entry gate. Existing one-time rewards acknowledge those reports; later narrative consequences are open for design.
  5. Push toward research. Continue the existing sample mission and extract. A future version could use the recording or household clues to make this leg more meaningful; no new dependency is assumed here.
Design guardrails: Keep the main objective understandable, make danger visible or audible, offer usable cover, and make alternate routes meaningful rather than mandatory. Test whether proposed returns are interesting before adding locked doors or extra travel.

Open creative decisions

  • What caused the district to be abandoned, and how much should the player understand at the start?
  • Who is the player, who receives the reports and why is the sample worth the risk?
  • How many homes and points of interest can we support at the intended quality?
  • Should the woodland trail eventually be a genuine second route to the relay, or a local exploration loop? Test feasibility and progression first.
  • How do the Listener and anomalies relate to the story, without explaining away their mystery?
